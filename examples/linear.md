## MVP idea

_A proposal of what to build. Nothing has been built._

- **Goal:** Test whether a live cross-team initiative roll-up, built on existing issues, projects, and initiatives, lets engineering leaders see status across teams without manual chasing.
- **Target user:** Engineering leaders and product managers at large engineering organizations replacing Jira (e7, e8).
- **Core job:** Coordination overhead that hides cross-team initiative status (e22, e8).

**Must-have features**

- **Cross-team initiative roll-up:** Shows initiatives with status aggregated from the teams working on them, the core of the job (r17, e8). `e8, r17, e1`
- **Teams and projects drill-down:** Lets a leader move from an initiative to the projects and issues behind it, so the roll-up can be checked (e1, e2). `e1, e2`
- **Shared planning objects:** Uses the existing issues, projects, cycles, and initiatives, so the MVP tests the same structure Linear already sells (e1, r4). `e1, r4`
- **Status changes visible to all:** Keeps the roll-up live as teams update issues, which is the claimed real-time view (e8, r11). `e8, r11`

**Out of scope**

- Pricing, plan tiers, and enterprise governance (r5, r7, r13)
- Non-engineering stakeholder views and reporting (r6)
- Marketing experiments and switch campaigns (a4 covers messaging)
- Jira migration tooling and agent-specific workflows (r8)

**Success criteria**

- Target users can find the status of a named initiative across at least two teams without asking anyone directly, in the prototype test.
- Interview participants say the roll-up would change how they run a Jira migration, recorded in the a5 notes.
- Users complete the demo scenario without help from the team.
- Results are reported per criterion, and any failure is recorded as a finding, not a pass.

**How a user tries it.** A director of engineering at a large organization opens the initiative roll-up for a planned launch. The view shows the launch as one initiative, with each contributing team's projects and their status rolled up beside it. She selects a project that is behind, drills into its open issues, and sees who owns each one. She then shares the view with a product manager, who sees the same live status without a separate status meeting. The team then records whether she says this would change how she runs the Jira migration, which tests the key assumption.
